# 01 — Market and Culture

## Market shape

The tiny home market is not one market. It splits by **structure type**, and the structure type determines everything about the land.

| Structure type | What it is | Land implications |
|---|---|---|
| **THOW** (tiny house on wheels) | Built on a trailer, often RVIA-certified, movable | Treated as an RV in most jurisdictions; needs RV-tolerant zoning, delivery access, hookups or off-grid systems |
| **Park model** | Factory-built, ANSI A119.5, up to ~400 sq ft, often on a chassis | RV-park and resort zoning; sometimes allowed where THOWs are not |
| **Site-built / foundation tiny home** | Built on a permanent foundation, often under IRC Appendix Q | Full residential permitting, septic, well, inspections, certificate of occupancy |
| **ADU / accessory dwelling** | Secondary unit on a lot with a main house | Urban and suburban infill; rental income; local ADU ordinances govern |
| **Converted structure** (skoolie, shouse, shed-to-house) | Bus, shed, barn conversion | Highly local; often legally ambiguous; insurance is the hard part |

### Buyer economics

- Most buyers are **middle-class, not low-income**. Many are cash-rich from selling a conventional home.
- Typical used THOW purchase: **$40k–$100k**. Custom builds: **$80k–$150k+**. DIY builds: **$20k–$50k** in materials.
- **Financing is the bottleneck.** Few lenders understand tiny homes. RVIA-certified THOWs can sometimes get RV loans. Foundation builds need local construction loans or cash.
- **Insurance is the second bottleneck.** Many carriers refuse THOWs outright. Address assignment, foundation type, and certification all affect insurability.
- Land budgets vary widely: **$15k–$75k** for a rural lot is common; off-grid buyers will pay more for acreage and water.

## Origins of the movement

The tiny home movement is a convergence of three strands:

1. **Minimalism and the "not so big" house** — Sarah Susanka's *The Not So Big House* (1998) argued for quality over quantity.
2. **The 2008 housing crash** — foreclosure, underwater mortgages, and distrust of 30-year debt pushed people toward outright ownership.
3. **The DIY / simple-living tradition** — Lloyd Kahn's *Tiny Homes: Simple Shelter* (2012) documented the handmade, self-built lineage.

Key figures and moments:
- **Jay Shafer** founded Tumbleweed Tiny House Company in the late 1990s and is widely treated as the movement's founder.
- **Tiny House Jamboree** (Colorado Springs, 2015) drew thousands and proved the market was real.
- **Spur, Texas** declared itself "tiny house friendly" in 2014 and became a symbolic destination.
- **TV wave**: *Tiny House Nation* (FYI, 2014), *Tiny House Hunters* (HGTV), plus YouTube channels like *Living Big in a Tiny House* and *Exploring Alternatives*.

## Core values

These are the beliefs that show up repeatedly in the community. They should inform brand voice, not just content.

- **Intentionality** — every object earns its square footage. "Less house, more life."
- **Financial freedom** — own outright, kill the mortgage, work less.
- **Experiences over possessions** — time and travel beat stuff.
- **Self-reliance and craft** — building it yourself is a moral good.
- **Environmental lightness** — small footprint, solar, composting, rainwater.
- **Skepticism of institutions** — HOAs, mortgages, zoning boards, consumerism.

## The four tribes

They share values but disagree about land.

### 1. THOW nomads
Mobility is the point. They want to move seasonally, boondock, and stay flexible. Land is a base camp or a parking spot, not a homestead.
- Wants: RV-tolerant zoning, easy in/out access, hookups or robust off-grid systems, storage for the trailer.
- Doesn't want: long-term commitments, HOAs, permanent foundations.

### 2. Foundation tiny home / ADU owners
Permanence and legitimacy. They want a legal, code-compliant dwelling, often with rental income.
- Wants: residential zoning, ADU ordinances, utilities, permits, financing, insurance.
- Doesn't want: gray-area legality, RV classification.

### 3. Off-grid homesteaders
Land-first. The house is one system among many (water, power, food, waste).
- Wants: acreage, solar exposure, water rights or catchment, septic or composting tolerance, privacy, room for garden/shop/animals.
- Doesn't want: HOAs, close neighbors, dependency on municipal services.

### 4. Tiny house community residents
They want neighbors and shared infrastructure without a conventional subdivision.
- Wants: legally recognized tiny home villages, shared utilities, community governance, services nearby.
- Doesn't want: isolation, regulatory battles.

## Shared vocabulary

Use these terms correctly. Getting them wrong signals outsider status.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **THOW** | Tiny house on wheels |
| **Skoolie** | Converted school bus |
| **Park model** | ANSI A119.5 factory-built unit, RV-adjacent |
| **NOAH certified** | National Organization of Alternative Housing certification for THOWs |
| **RVIA** | Recreation Vehicle Industry Association certification |
| **Appendix Q** | IRC appendix with prescriptive tiny-house provisions |
| **Shouse** | Shed-house conversion |
| **Boondocking** | Off-grid camping, no hookups |
| **Intentional community / eco-village** | Planned shared-living development |
| **Buildie** | Slang for a DIY tiny home builder |
| **Tiny house friendly** | A jurisdiction with permissive rules |

## Rituals and gathering places

- Tiny house jamborees and festivals (build tours, vendor halls, workshops)
- Build workshops and framing weekends
- Facebook groups, r/tinyhouses, Instagram and YouTube
- Meetups in "tiny house friendly" towns
- Pilgrimages to permissive jurisdictions (Spur, TX; select Colorado and California towns)

## The tensions — and why they matter to your product

These are the anxieties your buyers bring to the site. Address them directly and you win trust.

| Tension | What buyers feel | Product response |
|---|---|---|
| **The parking problem** | "I built it, now where do I legally put it?" | Make legality the core of search, not an afterthought |
| **Zoning gray zones** | "The county says maybe." | Plain-language status labels; "verify with county" CTAs |
| **Class critique** | "Tiny houses are a rich person's RV." | Show a range of budgets, including DIY and used |
| **Safety and livability** | Loft ladders, propane, condensation, composting | Honest guides; don't romanticize |
| **Insurance refusals** | "Nobody will insure it." | Insurance guidance per parcel type |
| **Commercialization** | The ethos vs. the $80k Instagram build | Lead with substance, not aesthetics |

## Tone of voice

**Do**
- Plain, specific, grounded. "This parcel is zoned A-1; tiny homes require a conditional use permit here."
- Name the constraint before the benefit.
- Respect the buyer's competence. Many are builders.
- Use the community's vocabulary correctly.
- Be honest about uncertainty.

**Don't**
- Use lifestyle-blog hype ("Live your best tiny life!").
- Promise legality you can't guarantee.
- Lead with aesthetics over feasibility.
- Talk down to DIY builders.
- Use "cheap" — use "affordable," "low-overhead," "debt-free."

**Voice in one line:** *A knowledgeable neighbor who has read the county code and will tell you the truth.*
