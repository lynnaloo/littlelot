## The ten things that should shape the product

1. **Buyers ask one question:** *"Can I legally and physically live here?"* Every feature should answer it faster.
2. **The parking problem is the product.** Where to legally put a tiny home is the movement's #1 pain point. Owning that question is the brand.
3. **Zoning is hyper-local and changes often.** Never present parcel legality as a guarantee. Trust is won with honesty about uncertainty.
4. **Internet is a hard requirement** for a large, high-value segment (remote workers). Treat it as a first-class filter, not a footnote.
5. **Delivery access is a hidden deal-breaker.** A THOW arrives on a trailer and weighs 10,000–20,000 lbs. Road width, grade, turn radius, and bridge limits matter.
6. **There are four distinct buyer tribes**, and they want different land. One generic listing page will underperform persona-specific pages.
7. **"No HOA" is a feature**, not a neutral data point. CC&Rs frequently ban tiny homes outright.
8. **The culture rewards transparency and punishes hype.** Plain language, real constraints, no Instagram fantasy.
9. **Financing is the bottleneck, not price.** Most buyers are middle-class and cash-rich from a home sale. Help with financing and insurance is a differentiator.
10. **Score, don't just filter.** A "Tiny Home Readiness Score" per parcel is more useful than 40 disconnected checkboxes.

## Suggested next steps

- Validate the scoring weights with 5–10 actual tiny home buyers
- Pick a parcel data vendor and confirm zoning coverage by county
- Build one persona page end-to-end as a pilot (recommend: Off-Grid Builder — highest intent, clearest land requirements)
- Draft the county-by-county zoning guide as the primary SEO and trust asset
